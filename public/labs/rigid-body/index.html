Bench notes

What should I look for?

Release the middle-axis rotor. Follow the orange marker as its alignment passes through zero and approaches −1. The dashed arrow always points along the initial angular momentum. The orange trail covers the last 1.5 seconds.

Switch to X or Z at the same instant. Their markers stay near the original direction. Set the nudge to zero and release again to isolate an exact principal-axis spin.

How is the rotor modeled?

Three uniform aluminum blocks form a head, spine and foot. Together they span 180 × 109 × 22 mm and weigh 407.268 g at 2,700 kg/m³. The center of mass lies 76.110 mm above the foot. Each axis is a principal axis of this shape.

The smallest moment is about X along the head. Y runs down the spine and Z is perpendicular to the broad face. Painted stripes, the marker and its trail carry no mass. Translation, friction and external torques are excluded.

How can I check the result?

The solver advances body angular velocity and a body-to-world quaternion together with fourth-order Runge–Kutta. Each completed step normalizes the quaternion. The energy check compares rotational kinetic energy to launch. The momentum check measures the change in all three world-frame components relative to initial magnitude.

At each launch the next body-coordinate component receives the selected fraction of the spin rate (X to Y, Y to Z, Z to X). Step advances exactly one 1/240 s interval. The benchmark repeats a 24 s run across three step sizes and compares an axisymmetric top to its closed-form solution.
